What the SAA-C03 exam expects you to design

Design Secure Architectures

Make every layer secure: identity, network, application, data, keys, and audit evidence.

What it means for the exam

  • Secure access. Pick IAM roles, federation, MFA, least privilege, and account guardrails instead of permanent credentials.
  • Secure workloads. Put compute in private subnets, expose only approved entry points, and filter traffic with security groups, NACLs, WAF, and endpoints.
  • Secure data. Encrypt at rest with KMS, encrypt in transit with TLS certificates, classify sensitive data, and define retention, backup, and replication policies.

Design Resilient Architectures

Keep applications available when traffic spikes, components fail, or an Availability Zone has problems.

What it means for the exam

  • Loose coupling. Use queues, topics, events, and workflows so one failing component does not stop the whole application.
  • High availability. Spread resources across multiple Availability Zones and use health checks, load balancing, and automatic replacement.
  • Disaster recovery. Match the answer to RTO and RPO: backup and restore is cheapest, active-active is fastest and usually most expensive.

What it means for the exam

  • Storage performance. Choose object, block, file, or hybrid storage based on latency, sharing, throughput, and access pattern.
  • Compute performance. Match EC2 family, Lambda memory, container platform, and scaling policy to the workload shape.

  • Analytics performance. Use Kinesis, Firehose, Glue, EMR, Athena, Redshift, and QuickSuite when the question describes ingestion, transformation, or reporting.

Design Cost-Optimized Architectures

Meet requirements at the lowest practical cost without breaking availability, performance, or security needs.

What it means for the exam

  • Storage cost. Use lifecycle rules, archive classes, right-sized volumes, and transfer methods that match access frequency.
  • Compute cost. Use Spot for interruptible jobs, Savings Plans or Reserved Instances for steady usage, and serverless for spiky demand.
  • Database cost. Pick the engine, capacity mode, retention period, cache layer, and replica strategy that meet the workload without overprovisioning.
  • Network cost. Watch cross-AZ, cross-Region, NAT gateway, internet, and edge-transfer paths; cheaper routing is often the exam clue.
